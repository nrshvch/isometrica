# Other games

What other games do with land, with towns and with many players on one map,
and what real towns do. Taken into account for the [design](README.md), not
copied from.

| Game             | Land and who owns it                                                                                                                                                        | Taken from it                                                                              |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Civilization     | A city's border grows with its culture, and tiles can be bought with gold, dearer the further out. Many players' cities on one map.                                         | The borders this game had before, and dropped: right for an empire, bookkeeping in a city. |
| Anno 1800        | An island is claimed by building a trading post on its coast, which takes influence by the island's size; one to an island. Up to four players, trading and taking islands. | Founding by building the first thing.                                                      |
| The Settlers II  | Land comes from military buildings with soldiers in them, and is won by conquest. Goods go along roads from flag to flag, carried.                                          | Roads as what everything moves along.                                                      |
| SimCity (2013)   | Up to sixteen cities in a region, sharing services; people go to work in, and pollution blows into, the cities round them.                                                  | Cities as neighbours who need each other.                                                  |
| OpenTTD          | Towns are nobody's. Each town rates every company, and building near it takes a good enough rating. What a network costs to keep grows faster than the network does.        | Permission rather than ownership.                                                          |
| Kingdoms Reborn  | A city builder for many players: cities of their own on one map, trading, and competing for land.                                                                           | The nearest to this game.                                                                  |
| Travian          | Settlers go to a free tile to found a village; if someone gets there first, they come back.                                                                                 | Racing for the good places.                                                                |
| Eco              | Land is claimed with stakes, five tiles by five, and others are let onto it.                                                                                                | Claims to protect, not to build.                                                           |
| Rust             | A base's upkeep goes up with how much of it there is, and a base nobody pays for decays.                                                                                    | Decay, and a world that tidies itself up.                                                  |
| Cities: Skylines | A road maintenance depot sends its trucks round the roads; in the first game the roads do not really wear.                                                                  | Road upkeep as a service.                                                                  |
| Foundation       | No grid and no set boundaries: villagers wear their own paths.                                                                                                              | Freedom to spread out.                                                                     |

None of these lets roads decide what belongs to which city.

## Real towns

- Towns grew up round a crossroads, a ford, a market, a mill: the first thing
  there was the reason for the town.
- Nikolaevsk, Alaska, was founded in 1968 by five Old Believer families, on
  land bought with a grant from the Tolstoy Foundation; they lived in tents
  at first.
- In much of Europe the sign where a road comes into a town is where its
  built-up part begins, and the speed limit with it; it is not the town's
  boundary on paper.
- In the United States, land outside any city often joins one to have its
  water and sewers: it belongs where it is connected.
- People choose a town by what it charges them and what it gives them,
  "voting with their feet" (Tiebout, 1956).
